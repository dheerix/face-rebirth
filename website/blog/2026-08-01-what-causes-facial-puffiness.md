Facial puffiness is common, changeable, and often temporary. Sleep, hydration shifts, salty meals, alcohol, allergies, crying, hormonal changes, and the position you sleep in can all influence how fluid gathers in facial tissues.

{/* truncate */}

## A few common contributors

Overnight, less movement and a horizontal sleeping position can make fluid more noticeable in the morning. Dehydration can also affect fluid balance, while allergy-related inflammation may be especially visible around the eyes.

## A gentle morning approach

Begin with water, easy neck and shoulder movement, and a few slow breaths. If touch feels comfortable, use light pressure rather than force. More intensity is not necessarily more effective.

Persistent, painful, one-sided, sudden, or breathing-related swelling deserves medical attention. This article is educational and is not a diagnosis.
